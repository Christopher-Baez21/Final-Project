Rick Rolling

History: Rickrolling is a classic internet bait-and-switch prank that originated in May 2007 on the anonymous imageboard 4chan. It evolved from an earlier site joke called "duckrolling"—where a word filter implemented by founder Christopher "moot" Poole mistakenly turned "eggroll" into "duckroll," prompting users to trick others into clicking links that led to a Photoshopped image of a duck on wheels. The true pivot to Rickrolling occurred when the highly anticipated trailer for the video game Grand Theft Auto IV crashed the Rockstar Games website due to intense traffic. Seizing the moment, a user named Shawn Cotter posted a disguised hyperlink promising a mirror link to the trailer, but instead redirected eager gamers to the music video for Rick Astley’s 1987 dance-pop hit "Never Gonna Give You Up". The gag exploded into a mainstream global phenomenon by 2008, finding its way into massive April Fools' Day stunts by YouTube, real-world protests by the hacktivist group Anonymous, and even a live performance by Rick Astley himself at the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade. Decades later, it remains one of the internet's most enduring, harmless, and universally recognized practical jokes.

Uses: Rickrolling is an internet bait-and-switch prank used primarily for harmless humor, digital misdirection, community bonding, and viral marketing. It involves tricking a person into clicking a hidden or disguised link that unexpectedly opens the music video for Rick Astley's 1987 hit song, "Never Gonna Give You Up". Impact: Rickrolling changed internet culture by creating a harmless, long-lasting bait-and-switch template and reviving the original pop singer’s career.
